# Admissions Queue

The **Admissions Queue** is the waiting line users see before they are let
into your game. Instead of every user hitting your backend at once, users
are enrolled into a queue and admitted at a rate you control. This is the same
mechanism large live-service titles use to survive a launch, a season drop, or
a free weekend — you decide how fast traffic arrives, and everyone else waits
their turn with a position in line.

<Note>
  The Admissions Queue is available on **Auth snap v1.14.0 and above**. If your
  Auth snap is on an older version, this tool will prompt you to upgrade.
</Note>

## Why use an admissions queue

When a game opens its doors, arrival traffic is spiky and does not resemble
steady-state load. Login, profile creation, inventory grants, and matchmaking
all fire at once. Left unmanaged, that spike is what takes a backend down at
the worst possible moment.

An admissions queue converts that spike into a flat, predictable line:

1. **Protect your backend.** You admit users at a rate your services and
   databases are known to handle, rather than whatever rate users happen to
   show up at.
2. **Stay open instead of going down.** Users in the queue see a position and
   a wait, which is a far better experience than failed logins or a hard error
   screen.
3. **Control your ramp.** Start conservative, watch your dashboards, and raise
   the rate as you gain confidence. Everything is changeable live.

## Pre-requisites

You need at least the **cluster-service-view** role with **Auth** service
access to view these settings, and update access to change them. If you do not
have the required permissions, please reach out to your company administrator
to upgrade your role.

## Tool Location

From your Snapend, open **Snaps Configuration**, select the **Auth** snap and
click **Admissions Queue** in the side navigation.

The tool reads your Snapend's current admissions queue configuration when it
loads. Change any of the values below and click **Save** to apply them.

## Enabling the queue

The **Enable Admissions Queue** toggle is the master switch.

- **Off** — this is today's behavior. Users log in exactly as they do now and
  are never put in line. Nothing is enrolled and the queue consumer does
  nothing.
- **On** — arriving users are enrolled into the queue and admitted at the
  consume rate you set.

You can save the rate settings below while the queue is off, so you can dial in
a configuration ahead of a launch and then flip the switch when you are ready.

<Note>
  Turning the queue off releases everyone who is currently waiting. Only turn it
  off when you are confident your backend can absorb the users still in line.
</Note>

## Admission Rates

Two settings work together to control throughput. Both are expressed in
**users per second**.

### Max Rate

The ceiling admission rate — a hard backstop. The queue will never admit faster
than this, no matter what the consume rate is set to. Pick a value your backend
has actually been load tested at and leave it alone. Think of it as the fuse in
the circuit, not the dial you turn during an event.

### Consume Rate

The rate you are actually admitting at right now. This must be between `0` and
the max rate, and it is **live changeable** — you can raise or lower it during
an event without a redeploy and the queue picks up the new rate immediately.

This is the dial your live-ops team turns:

- **Ramp up slowly** at the start of a launch window, then increase as your
  dashboards stay healthy.
- **Set it to `0`** to pause admissions entirely while keeping everyone in line
  and holding their position. This is the lever to pull during an incident —
  users keep waiting instead of getting kicked out.
- **Raise it to the max rate** once the initial rush has passed and the queue
  has drained.

<Warning>
  The consume rate cannot exceed the max rate. If you need to admit faster than
  your current ceiling, raise the max rate first — and only after you are
  confident your backend can take it.
</Warning>

## Burst Cap

Admissions are metered by a shared token bucket. Tokens accrue at the consume
rate, and each admitted user spends one. The **burst cap** is the maximum
number of tokens that bucket can hold, which caps how large a group can be
admitted at once after a quiet period.

- Match it to your consume rate to allow a one second burst. That is the
  smoothest setting and a good default.
- Raise it to allow a larger clump of users through at once — useful if your
  backend handles batches more efficiently than a perfectly even trickle.

A burst cap of `600` at a consume rate of `60` users/sec means the queue can
release roughly ten seconds worth of admissions in one go if it has been idle.

<Warning>
  The burst cap must be at least `1`. A burst cap of `0` is not a valid
  configuration, and the tool will not let you save one.
</Warning>

## Reconnect Grace

Waiting users hold a ticket. **Reconnect grace** is the silent window, in
seconds, that a ticket survives without hearing from the user before it is
reaped and their spot released.

Set this long enough to survive the everyday interruptions — a brief network
drop, a phone call, an app being backgrounded — so users do not lose their
place through no fault of their own. Set it short enough that spots abandoned
by users who gave up are freed for the people still waiting. A value around
`30` seconds is a reasonable starting point.

<Note>
  A reconnect grace of `0` reaps a ticket as soon as a user goes silent. That
  is almost never what you want on a mobile title, where brief disconnects are
  routine.
</Note>

## Bypass APIs

While the queue is enabled, a user who is still waiting in line gets a `403`
from your backend. That is the whole point — it is what stops the rush from
reaching your services before you are ready for it.

But a client stuck on a queue screen usually still needs to do *something*.
It may want to poll its position in line, show the store front, pull down a
news feed or patch notes, or read remote config so the waiting screen renders
correctly. **Bypass APIs** is where you nominate the endpoints that stay
reachable while a user waits. Everything you list here answers normally;
everything else keeps returning the `403` until the user is admitted.

### Nominating endpoints

The widget works the same way as the one in the
[API Key Manager](/docs/infra/snaps/auth/config-tools/api-key-manager):

1. <u>Available Snaps</u> - Pick the snaps you want to exempt. **Select All
   Snaps** adds every snap on your Snapend at once. Removing a snap from this
   list removes its bypass rule entirely.
2. <u>All Endpoints or Custom Endpoints</u> - A newly added snap starts as
   **All Endpoints**, meaning every API on it is exempt. Click **+ Custom
   Endpoints** to narrow it down instead, which opens the API picker. Select
   the endpoints you want and confirm.
   <Note>
     In the API picker widget, do not forget to click the "Confirm" button to
     save your selection.
   </Note>
3. <u>Removing an endpoint</u> - Each selected endpoint has a delete button
   next to it. Removing an endpoint puts it back behind the queue.

Switching a snap from Custom Endpoints back to **All Endpoints** discards the
individual endpoints you picked, so the tool asks you to confirm first.

The picker lists the user-authenticated APIs on each snap, because those are
the ones the queue gates. Your login and auth APIs are not in the list — a
user has to be able to reach those before they can be put in line at all.

Your BYOSnaps are not in the list either — they gate themselves. See
[BYOSnaps and the admitted-at header](#byosnaps-and-the-admitted-at-header)
below for how that works.

<Note>
  Endpoints are matched on their operation, not on their URL. That means a
  bypass rule keeps working across a path change, and it also means the method
  and URL shown in the list are there to make the entry readable rather than
  to control what matches.
</Note>

### BYOSnaps and the admitted-at header

A [BYOSnap](/docs/custom/bring-your-own-snap) is your own code, running in the
Snapser cluster alongside the platform snaps. Snapser does not hold BYOSnap
traffic back for the queue — it cannot reasonably decide on your behalf which
of your endpoints a waiting user should be allowed to call.

BYOSnaps handle their own gating instead, which is why they are left out of
**Available Snaps**: that list drives the shared admission middleware, and your
BYOSnap does not run it.

Instead, every request forwarded to a BYOSnap carries an **`admitted-at`**
header, and your handler decides what to do with it:

| `admitted-at` | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `0` | The caller has not been admitted through the queue. They are either still waiting in line, or they were never enrolled because the queue is off. |
| A UTC timestamp | The caller cleared the queue, at that time. |

So the decision the Bypass APIs list makes for platform snaps is one you make
in code for your own:

- **Reject on `0`** for anything that should wait its turn. Returning a `403`
  keeps your BYOSnap consistent with how the rest of the Snapend behaves.
- **Serve it anyway** for the handful of endpoints your queue screen genuinely
  needs, exactly as you would add them to the bypass list.

A useful pattern is to mirror the list above in your own code: keep one
allowlist of handlers that are safe to serve to a waiting user, and have
everything else check the header and turn away callers that have not been
admitted.

<Note>
  Every request carries `admitted-at: 0` while the queue is off, because nobody
  is being enrolled. Code that rejects on `0` alone will therefore turn away
  all of your traffic the moment the queue is disabled — make sure your check
  only applies when the queue is actually enabled.
</Note>

### Keep the list short

Every API you exempt is traffic that reaches your backend before the queue has
throttled it. That is a deliberate hole in the protection you just set up, so
it is worth being strict about what goes in:

- **Prefer individual endpoints over All Endpoints.** Exempting a whole snap
  is convenient, but it also exempts every API that snap gains in the future,
  including ones you have never load tested against launch traffic.
- **Favor cheap reads.** A cached news feed or a remote config lookup costs
  you very little. A write path, a matchmaking call, or anything that fans out
  to several services is exactly what the queue exists to defer.
- **Revisit it after a launch.** An endpoint you exempted to debug a bad
  launch window does not need to stay exempt forever.

<Warning>
  Saving replaces the entire bypass list with what is on screen. It is not a
  merge, so anything removed from the list stops being exempt the moment you
  save.
</Warning>

If you set a snap to **Custom Endpoints** and then remove every endpoint under
it, the tool will block **Save** and tell you which snap is empty. Either pick
at least one endpoint, switch it back to All Endpoints, or remove the snap
from the list.

## Bypass Users

Where Bypass APIs exempts *what* can be called, **Bypass Users** exempts *who*
is calling. A user ID listed here is never put in the queue at all — they log
in and play immediately, however long the line is.

Enter a comma separated list of user IDs. Blanks and duplicates are ignored,
and pasting a column of IDs one per line works too.

This is for the accounts that have to be able to get in during a launch:

- **QA and live-ops**, so they can verify the build and work an incident while
  the queue is doing its job.
- **Support**, so an agent can reproduce what a user is reporting.
- **Partners and creators** you have committed to, where being stuck in line is
  a business problem rather than a technical one.

<Warning>
  Every bypassed user is load the queue is not smoothing out. This list is for a
  handful of internal accounts — it is not a way to let a segment of your users
  skip the line, and a long list defeats the point of running a queue at all.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Bypass Users is independent of Bypass APIs. A bypassed user is never queued,
  so they reach every endpoint normally and the bypass endpoint list does not
  come into it.
</Note>

## Recommended starting configuration

| Setting | Suggested value | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enabled | Off until launch day | Configure ahead of time, flip it on when you need it |
| Max Rate | Your load tested ceiling | A backstop you never expect to hit |
| Consume Rate | 25–50% of max rate | Ramp up as your dashboards stay healthy |
| Burst Cap | Match your consume rate | Allows a one second burst, the smoothest admission |
| Reconnect Grace | `30` | Survives a brief drop without holding dead spots |
| Bypass APIs | Only what the queue screen needs | Every exempt API is un-throttled traffic |
| Bypass Users | Your QA and live-ops accounts | Enough to work a launch, not enough to matter as load |

Every game is different — treat these as a place to start, then tune against
your own load tests.
