# Alert Manager

## Concept

Snapser continuously evaluates the health of every Snapend you run — CPU,
memory, error rate, pod restarts and more. When a condition trips, Snapser
fires a **platform alert**. The Alert Manager is where you decide _where_
those alerts get delivered and _which_ snapends you care about.

Alert Manager is a game-level tool. Channels and subscriptions live at the
app level and can fan out across any Snapend in that app — dev, staging or
production.

If you instead want to see the alerts that are firing **right now** on a
single Snapend, head to the [Snapend Alerts](/docs/snapend/alerts) viewer.

<Note>
  Alerts are catalog-driven. You can subscribe to any alert in the Snapser
  catalog but you cannot author custom alerts from the portal today — reach
  out to support if there's a signal you'd like us to surface.
</Note>

## Want alerts on the Snapser web portal?

By default, the [Snapend Alerts](/docs/snapend/alerts) viewer in
**Infrastructure → Alerts** is **empty** — even if alerts are firing on
your snapend. To make alerts show up there, you need to:

1. Create a **Portal** channel from the Alert Manager (zero configuration
   — just hit **+ Channel**, leave the type on `Portal`, and save).
2. Create a **Subscription** that ties the Portal channel to the snapends
   and catalog alerts you care about.

Without these two steps, the in-product Alerts viewer stays empty and the
snapend-header alert badge will never light up. You can still receive
alerts via Slack, Email or PagerDuty without a Portal channel — those
delivery paths are independent.

## Pre-requisites

You need at least the `game-view` role to open the Alert Manager. To create
or edit channels and subscriptions, you need a role that grants `PUT` on the
game scope (`game-edit` or higher). Users without write access still see the
page but in a read-only view with a banner explaining the restriction.

## Tool Location

From your app home page, open the left nav and click **Tools → Alert
Manager**. The page opens on the **Subscriptions** tab by default — once
setup is done that's the screen you'll come back to most often.

![Alert Manager](/images/docs/alert-manager-overview.png)

Both `+ Channel` and `+ Subscription` are visible in the top-right at all
times. `+ Subscription` is disabled until you've created at least one
channel — there has to be somewhere for alerts to land before a subscription
makes sense.

## Channels

A **channel** is a destination Snapser pushes alerts to. Channels are
configured once per game and reused across any number of subscriptions.
Snapser supports four types today:

| Type | What you provide | Verification |
|------|------------------|--------------|
| **Portal** | Nothing — zero configuration. Subscribing a snapend to a Portal channel makes its alerts appear in the in-product Alerts viewer | None — auto-verified at creation |
| **Slack** | A Slack Web API URL (defaults to `https://slack.com/api/chat.postMessage`), a bot token and a comma-separated list of channel names | None — works as soon as you hit Test |
| **PagerDuty** | A PagerDuty integration key | None — works as soon as you hit Test |
| **Email** | A destination email address | Verified manually by Snapser staff before alerts can be delivered |

<Note>
  Adding a second Portal channel does the same thing as the first — the
  form will nudge you toward editing the existing one rather than creating
  a duplicate. (We don't block the create; the warning is informational.)
</Note>

### Adding a channel

Click **+ Channel**. The inline form drops in between the header and the
tabs. Pick a type, fill in the fields and hit **Save**. The channel
appears in the **Channels** table.

![Add Channel](/images/docs/alert-manager-add-channel.png)

### Verified vs Enabled

Channels carry two independent flags:

1. **Verified** — Snapser confirms this channel is allowed to receive
   alerts. Portal, Slack and PagerDuty are auto-verified at creation;
   email channels start unverified and need manual approval by Snapser
   staff.
2. **Enabled** — _you_ control whether this channel is currently
   delivering. Turn it off to pause without deleting the configuration.

Both flags must be true for an alert to be delivered. The Channels table
shows a chip for each so you can see at a glance why a channel isn't firing.

<Note>
  The two flags are deliberately decoupled. You can leave a verified
  channel disabled while you migrate destinations, and an unverified email
  channel can still be marked enabled in advance — alerts will start
  flowing the moment Snapser flips Verified to true.
</Note>

### Testing a channel

Each row in the Channels table has a **Test** action that sends a synthetic
alert to that channel only. Use it whenever you change a destination, key
or token. The button is disabled until the channel is both verified and
enabled.

<Note>
  Portal channels don't expose a Test action — there's no remote
  destination to confirm, so testing is a no-op. The first real alert
  that fires on a subscribed snapend will show up in
  **Infrastructure → Alerts** on its own.
</Note>

## Subscriptions

A **subscription** ties three things together:

1. One or more **channels** — where the alert is delivered.
2. One or more **snapends** — which clusters the subscription applies to.
3. One or more **catalog alerts** — which signals you care about.

A single alert firing on a covered snapend will be delivered to every
channel in every matching subscription. There's no priority or override
system — every match fires.

### Adding a subscription

Click **+ Subscription**. The form exposes three multi-select pickers
(Channels, Snapends, Alerts) plus two toggles:

- **Notify on resolve** — send a follow-up message when the alert clears.
  Off by default to keep low-severity noise down.
- **Enabled** — global on/off switch for the subscription. Same semantics
  as the channel flag — leave a subscription configured but paused.

![Add Subscription](/images/docs/alert-manager-add-subscription.png)

Each picker has a **Select all** shortcut next to it.

<Note>
  All three pickers stay open while you tick boxes, so you can pick several
  values without having to re-open the dropdown each time.
</Note>

### Filtering subscriptions

When a game has many snapends, the Subscriptions table can get noisy. Use
the filter icon in the **Snapends** column header to narrow the table down
to subscriptions covering a specific cluster (or set of clusters). Long
chip lists in any cell collapse to a `+N` button that opens the full list
in a popover on click.

## Severity model

Catalog alerts carry one of three severities:

| Severity | What it means |
|----------|---------------|
| `critical` | Customer-impacting — a snap or pod is down, hot, or hitting an unrecoverable error |
| `warning` | Something to look at soon — elevated error rates, restart counts, latency drift |
| `info` | Informational — useful telemetry, not actionable on its own |

Severity flows through to the Snapend Alerts viewer and to the badge on
the Snapend header so the highest active severity always wins the color
treatment.

## Lifecycle

Once an alert fires, Snapser stores it in a short-lived buffer so the
[Snapend Alerts](/docs/snapend/alerts) viewer can show it without having
to re-query Grafana. Entries auto-clear after 30 minutes and re-appear
automatically if the underlying issue is still active. Channel delivery
is independent of this buffer — the subscription fires on every Grafana
evaluation cycle that produces a `firing` event.

## FAQs

### Why didn't I get notified?

Walk down this checklist:

1. Is the channel both **verified** and **enabled**?
2. Is there a subscription that covers the snapend the alert fired on?
3. Is the subscription **enabled**?
4. Is the catalog alert that fired in the subscription's alert list?

If all four are true and you're still not getting notifications, contact
support — we can replay the delivery from the server-side log.

### Can I silence a single alert temporarily?

Yes — either disable the subscription, or remove the specific catalog
alert from the subscription's alert list. There's no time-bound silence
feature today.

### Can I author my own alert rules?

Not from the portal. The catalog is curated by Snapser. If you'd like a
signal added, reach out to support with a description of the condition
and severity you want.

### Do channels work across environments?

Yes — channels are game-scoped and can be subscribed to from dev, staging
or production snapends through the **Snapends** picker on a subscription.

### What happens if Snapser staff rejects my email channel?

The channel stays in the table with `verified: false` and a tooltip
explaining the rejection. You can edit the destination and try again.

### My snapend Alerts viewer is empty — why?

The viewer only displays alerts for snapends that are subscribed to a
**Portal** channel. If you haven't created a Portal channel yet, or you
have one but no subscription routes this snapend to it, the viewer will
stay empty even while alerts are firing. Head to the Alert Manager,
create a Portal channel if you don't have one, and add (or edit) a
subscription that covers this snapend.

### Can I have more than one Portal channel?

Yes — the create form will warn you that a second Portal channel is
redundant (it delivers to the same in-product viewer as the first), but
it won't block the create. In most cases the simpler thing is to edit
or disable the existing Portal channel rather than make a new one.
